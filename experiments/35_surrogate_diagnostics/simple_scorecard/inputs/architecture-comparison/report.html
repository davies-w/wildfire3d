How well do the models predict 16 seconds ahead?

Three measured comparisons. No four-state MLP was trained.
Three-state models predict water, dry fuel and vegetation fuel-gas release. The four-state GRU also predicts a surface-heating index—not heat energy.

Each forecast starts from observed history and then uses its own predictions for eight two-second steps. These are test-fold averages, grouped by the simulation’s activity over that whole forecast interval.

Activity definition: trailing nominal10seconds, ≥0.1% initial dry-fuel-equivalent release or initial water loss per10seconds. Quiet: both below0.01%. Intermediate rates are weak residual. Every forecast step must share the category to count as stable; otherwise it is a transition. Active includes drying, not necessarily combustion. Trailing smoothing delays labels. Thresholds are engineering tolerances, not physically validated extinction criteria.

Equal mean across available test folds, with configurations and replicas balanced inside each fold. Same520histories/480configurations; historically examined and reshuffled test data. Separate experiments combine architecture and state changes; not a full factorial controlled study. Local inference only; whole-history error replay agreed with saved GPU results within0.3%.
