## Question
Can shared local state-transition and heat-transfer models, learned from small FDS configurations including building surfaces, compose into larger unseen arrangements without full-scene training labels or FDS inputs at inference?

This is a feasibility test against FDS, not validation of real wildfire risk. The previous experiment's whole-scene ignition model is not reused as the learned physics.

## Deliberate simplifications
- Single generic particle-vegetation material, not oak/eucalyptus identification; retain documented reference material parameters. No claim that scaling reference vegetation to a mature crown validates its combustion. Fuel-loading sensitivity required; mass and moisture stated in every deck/report. Initial sensitivity multipliers 0.5, 1, 2 of the reference dry fuel density; initial moisture 0.14 dry-mass basis, verify deck interpretation before launch.
- One synthetic combustible building surface: existing 20 mm wood layer and prescribed 350 C ignition/200 kW m-2 burning law. This is a proxy, not realistic stucco/roof assemblies or validated pyrolysis. No interior fire, windows, vents, structural collapse, brands, fences or decks in this first feasibility test. Eaves are an assigned feature.
- Flat terrain, fixed 3 m/s free-stream wind with two orientations (normal/oblique), dry weather; no terrain-driven flow. Geometry extraction does not claim a faithful property reconstruction.
- External ignition patches beneath vegetation, explicitly metered and ramped for 20 seconds. Source strengths are chosen in a development-only bracket to give partial heating and burning; source contribution reported separately. Bare-source controls detect direct burner heating. Predictions tested after the source turns off as well as during it.
- 180-second physical horizon, not a complete wildfire event. Negatives are censored at 180 s. Default uniform 0.5 m cells; selected 0.25 m reruns test whether the chosen reference is credible. No assumption that 0.5 m resolves every ignition mechanism.
- Compact typical domains approximately 24 x 24 x 24 m (110,592 cells), expanding as required for building length, crown or buffer. Larger tests up to 48 x 48 x 24 m (442,368 cells). A narrow crown-to-boundary gap is not accepted merely to meet these sizes.
- Retain particles and numerical diagnostics, boundary/slice evidence and provenance. Full volume imagery only for one demonstration; do not spend on imagery for every training run.

## Learned interface
Nodes carry material/geometry descriptors and state: vegetation remaining mass and thermal state; facade temperature-profile representation and burning state. Shared transition models predict state increments and emitted heat over a 0.5 s interval. Edges predict received radiative and convective flux, conditioned on relative geometry, sender state, background wind and neighbouring geometry. The building/corner context is an input, not ignored.

Training targets are local FDS histories: received radiation/convection, temperatures, mass-loss/burning rates and subsequent states. Instrument incoming versus self-emitted radiation explicitly; verify FDS quantity/sign conventions before launching. Do not silently replace incoming flux with net flux. If temperature-profile or fuel-state diagnostics cannot be extracted faithfully, revise the interface before spending on the dataset.

At inference, external inputs are initial conditions and prescribed ignition histories ONLY. No test FDS temperatures, heat fluxes, or local velocity fields are supplied. The graph predicts transport and states recursively. The transport model must account for building-induced flow approximately from geometry; its adequacy is part of the test, not an assumed capability. Non-neighbour radiation edges are included using visibility/distance, not arbitrary nearest-neighbour-only connections. Compare with an otherwise identical model lacking learned neighbour interactions and with a simple additive heat-transfer baseline. Monitor nonnegative mass and bounded energy; do not call ad hoc clamps physical conservation.

## Prespecified success criteria (engineering targets, not validated safety tolerances)
A. Context/refinement screen: cumulative positive incident heat at each materially exposed receiver changes <=10%; temperature-history MAE <=25 C; no ignition-label flip, and ignition-time difference <=5 s when both ignite. A materially exposed receiver accumulates >=100 kJ/m2 positive incident heat; also report absolute error for every other patch. Failing any screen stops expansion.
B. Held-out autonomous composition: >=14/16 correct ignition outcomes, zero false negatives; at least 4 positive and 4 negative FDS outcomes, otherwise inconclusive. The 16 runs are correlated seed repeats, not 16 independent properties.
C. On exposed surfaces: median per-surface normalized cumulative-heat error <=20% with denominator max(reference dose,100 kJ/m2); 90th percentile <=40%; temperature-history MAE <=30 C. Median ignition-time error <=10 s for true positives; report missed ignitions separately, never exclude them from classification.
D. Learned interaction model reduces test heat-dose MAE by >=20% versus the additive baseline and does not worsen ignition classification. Report all training seeds, not just the best.
E. Whole 180 s graph rollout <1 s per held-out scene on a single CPU thread including feature assembly, excluding model load; report actual graph size and single-scene latency. Learning curves must improve or show clearly where more data is needed. Count FDS generation + training cost separately; this pilot cannot prove the asymptotic cost of a general library.
