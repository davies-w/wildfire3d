## Model-design audit and local alternative probe
Original37 frozen artifacts verified unchanged. Scripts: audit_model_assumptions.py, audit_target_conventions.py, probe_shared_rom.py. No new simulations or cloud spending.

Confirmed design issues:
- window_loss supplies the exact FDS state and observed heat at every step, so state errors do not feed into the next training state. Only hidden memory/rate recur within8-step windows; hidden memory and emitted rate reset to zero at arbitrary window starts. Autonomous rollout receives neither correction.
- Pair adaptation freezes the response/output parameters. Because observed heat drives the response during fitting, pair state/fuel losses do not provide the transport parameters the end-to-end response gradient needed to optimize autonomous dynamics. The direct heat loss still trains transport; it is not zero-gradient training overall.
- The imposed temperature increment bound conflicts with2280/68400 transitions across12 isolated training cases under observed heat. A_1 has178 such transitions; largest increment excess607.16C. Thus some one-step teacher-forced targets cannot be matched at any parameter setting. Lower clamp20C also excludes measured evaporative cooling (B_1 minimum19.188C). This small floor mismatch does not explain hundreds of degrees of overall error.
- Empty regions default to20C in extraction, but there are ZERO empty samples in these12 single-tree training cases. This convention is not their failure cause.
- Tree fuel release is immediately converted to heat sent through a positive, distance-based kernel with no resolved air/gas/flame state, oxygen dependence, occlusion or energy budget. Solid temperature/remaining wet+solid mass and8 hidden values stand in for moisture, internal temperature and chemical history. These are unsupported closure assumptions, not calibrated physics.
- The actual ring of1600 heater particles becomes one point with inverse-distance attenuation and two learned gains. Wind/moisture are fixed in the pilot and absent as variable model inputs. Equal volume/16 descriptors do not represent the unequal cone height-slice volumes.

Alternative probe: POD compression followed by regularized linear dynamics with delayed latent history and heater input (a reduced-order system-identification baseline). It is not an implementation of a proven wildfire solver. One-trajectory fits for A/B/C standard exposures reach temperature MAE57.20/0.016/26.17C, but bases/operators were selected on the same trajectory and can violate mass bounds; those are memorization diagnostics only.

More informative A-only shared probe: basis/operator fitted on four training exposures, rank/delay/ridge selected on two existing validation exposures. Selected rank12, four latent-history samples, ridge.001. It advances autonomously from known20C/full-mass initial conditions plus heater schedule. No trajectory feedback. Mean regional temperature MAE55.16C train /55.82C validation, versus original singles-trained shared graph266.42C /261.61C on these same A configurations. Mass-fraction MAE .1435/.2569, versus graph .378/.318. The baseline specializes to A; original graph was shared across A/B/C. Different scope, output targets and parameterization prevent claiming a general architecture win. No test cases entered fitting. Existing validation has been used for selection; no new independent success claim. The model does not enforce mass bounds or predict transport/flame fields, so it is not ready for composition.

Recommended direction: first repair targets/masks and train multi-step autonomous rollouts; separate response-to-local-heating identification from prediction of that heating. Compare a constrained reduced-order state-space model with a learned latent dynamical model on identical development splits. A composable component should accept spatial/time-dependent incoming radiation and local gas/flow conditions and emit fuel/heat/state updates; include a reduced representation of surrounding air to close the feedback loop. Use existing gas/surface data to test this representation before requesting new simulations. Revisit graph assembly only after single-component and familiar-pair rollouts meet declared heat/state tolerances. Do not simply enlarge the old kernel or interpret successful replay as transfer.

Research basis:
- Sanchez-Gonzalez et al., Learning to Simulate Complex Physics with Graph Networks (2020), https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/sanchez-gonzalez20a.html : learned physical-state message passing and explicit attention to rollout error; not evidence that this canopy graph is valid.
- Peherstorfer & Willcox, Data-driven operator inference for nonintrusive projection-based model reduction (2016), https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/6120d5d1-6aa7-4437-a451-4ab5fbdac5d9 : reduced operators inferred from trajectories, with specific model/data assumptions.
- McGrattan et al., Modeling the Burning of Complicated Objects Using Lagrangian Particles, https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=905798 : vegetation particles couple to gas conservation/heat transfer; spatial heat-release distribution matters.
- Lu et al., DeepONet (2021), https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-021-00302-5 : input-function to output-function learning is relevant to component boundary-response models, but does not guarantee composability, conservation or accuracy with this small dataset.

### Conditional heat-response diagnostic (local, no new solver runs)

`train_heat_response.py` fits a separate recurrent response for each fixed canopy type using four isolated cases and selects checkpoints on two isolated validation cases. Each model receives recorded incident radiation and net convective flux, then rolls its own temperature and remaining mass forward for 180 s at 2 s steps; it also predicts four fuel-vapor release rates. No pair is fitted. Weights and script were hashed before evaluating the existing AA/AB/BB development pairs and source-removal controls (`results/heat-response-freeze.json`). These are development diagnostics, not a new untouched test.

Mean isolated-validation regional temperature errors are A 12.62°C, B 0.87°C, C 17.49°C; corresponding mass-fraction errors are 0.029, 0.001, 0.029. Errors averaged over active regions (either prediction or reference above 100°C) are much higher: 53.30, 11.55, 55.69°C. B validation is predominantly weak heating. Each model has roughly 32,000 parameters, trained from only four configurations: capacity and memorization remain substantial concerns.

Conditional pair results isolate an unresolved response problem. AA source cumulative fuel is 31.33 kg predicted versus 29.91 kg FDS; AB training source 32.19 versus 31.81 kg; AB validation source 32.05 versus 31.82 kg. But AA receiver fuel is 0.970 versus 0.0227 kg, with 21.47°C all-region and 127.09°C active-region temperature MAE. AB training receiver is 0.0872 versus 0.000612 kg; AB validation receiver 0.1079 versus 0.000832 kg. The receiver's small fuel release is therefore badly overpredicted despite receiving measured heat. All cumulative values here use the same left-sampled 2 s integration; original high-frequency devices remain preserved.

The constraints ensure nonincreasing bounded mass, nonnegative temperature and fuel release no greater than predicted wet/solid mass loss. They do NOT distinguish evaporation from pyrolysis adequately: low-temperature mass loss can still be allocated to fuel. This is a concrete architectural allowance consistent with the false receiver fuel, not proof it is the only cause. Isolated training only varied a bottom-ring artificial heater; lateral receiver heating is a different spatial exposure. A whole-canopy dense decoder is not a shared local material-response rule. These are the next design issues to address before autonomous transport.

This is conditional response, NOT FDS-free composition: net convective flux depends on true FDS particle temperatures, and no independent air/flame solver is provided. Fuel rate times 17,425 kJ/kg is potential chemical power, not actual gas-phase combustion or neighbor heat deposition. A next response interface should distinguish moisture evaporation and combustible fuel loss and test a spatially shared material update; autonomous coupling also needs gas temperature/velocity and radiation prediction, rather than supplying measured net convection.

Setup remains generic fixed particle canopies (A: 8 m-high cone, radius 1.5 m; B: 5 m-high cone, radius 2.5 m; C: 8 m-high cylinder, radius 1.5 m; all bases 2 m), 0.5 m grid, 20×20×15 m domain, wind 3 m/s in +x, moisture 0.12 kg/kg dry. Artificial 1500°C hot-particle initialization varies 10–25 s in singles, 20 s in pairs. Bare heatable nonflammable ground; no trunks, buildings, ground fuel or embers. This diagnoses the learned response to this FDS setup, not physical validation.
